Root cause 4 of 5

"I don't feel like I belong in this room."

Solution: nurture positive relationships. Felt safety is a prerequisite for sustained focus, not a separate issue from it.

A brief moment of real connection clears some of the "am I okay here" background noise before academic focus is asked for.

30-Second Buddy Check-In

Turn-and-tell a partner one thing before independent work begins — a highlight from lunch, a one-word mood, or a quick recall answer.

Consistent Greeting Ritual at the Door

A specific handshake, phrase, or fist-bump as students enter — same one, every day. Relational and functional at once: it's also a transition cue.

Collaborative Pattern Puzzle

A 16-word grid, sort into four categories of four, done in pairs instead of solo. Same cognitive-flexibility benefit, plus a shared "we figured this out together" moment.

Shout-Out Round

Thirty seconds, one or two students name something a classmate did well recently — academic or otherwise. Reinforces that the room notices people, not just answers.

Flip the Lens

Give a short, generic two-sentence conflict scenario (not a real classroom incident). Students rewrite it in first person from the other person's point of view. Builds the empathy and perspective-taking that a room needs before it can feel safe.

Pairs well with Cognitive Load: the door-greeting ritual works as a transition cue too — one predictable moment doing double duty.
