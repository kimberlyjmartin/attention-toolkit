Root cause 1 of 5

"I don't know what to do, or how to do it."

Solution: reduce cognitive load. These warm-ups strip away everything competing for working memory, so students spend their attention on the task itself — not on figuring out the task.

The goal isn't to make content easier. It's to make the format so predictable that students never have to think about "what am I supposed to do right now" before they can start actually thinking.

Visual Lesson Roadmap

Post 2–3 icons or steps on the board showing what today's session includes, visible the moment students walk in. Working memory doesn't have to hold "what's coming next" in reserve — it can just glance at the map.

Same-Seat, Same-Spot Bell-Ringer

A consistent physical location for the daily warm-up — same corner of the board, same handout tray — removes a decision point before the thinking even begins.

One-Step-Visible Instructions

Reveal one instruction at a time instead of a full paragraph of directions. Reduces the "which step am I on" load that eats into actual task focus.

The 3-2-1 Warm-Up Card

A pre-printed, identical-format card every day: 3 things I remember from yesterday, 2 questions I have, 1 word for how I feel about today's topic. Same structure, zero overhead spent on format.

Consistent Multisensory Cue

Pair the same sound and gesture every time a warm-up begins — a chime plus a hand signal. After a few weeks, the cue itself starts triggering the focused state.

Pairs well with Belonging: the multisensory cue above is also a relational ritual — same signal, same sense of "this is our routine," every day.
