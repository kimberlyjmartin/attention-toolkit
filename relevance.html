Root cause 2 of 5

"I don't find this meaningful, relevant, or interesting."

Solution: incorporate student background knowledge. Attention follows relevance — these warm-ups hook curiosity or personal connection before asking for sustained effort.

None of these need to be profound. They just need to activate something students already know or feel before the lesson asks them to build on top of it.

The Curiosity Gap

Project a macro or close-up photo of an everyday object as students walk in. Two minutes: guess and defend what it is. The reveal delivers a small dopamine hit tied to prediction, which carries forward into instruction.

"Why Does This Matter to Me?" Quick-Connect

One sentence, written or shared aloud: how does today's topic touch something in your own life? Activates existing schema instead of starting cold.

Retrieval Practice as the Warm-Up

Open with 2–3 recall questions from yesterday's material, no notes. Pulling information out of memory strengthens it more than review does, and it bridges directly into today's content.

Object or Image Tied to the Day's Text

Display a physical object or image connected to the reading — not explained yet. Students write predictions about the connection. Meaning-making starts before the lesson does.

Two-Pass Riddle

Give a genuinely hard riddle and 90 seconds, then deliberately shift to something else — attendance, the agenda — for about two minutes before returning to the same riddle for a final 90 seconds. Naming the effect for students ("your brain kept working on it") makes retrieval visible as a skill, not a trick.

Pairs well with Modality: the Curiosity Gap works through a visual channel — swap in a sound clip instead of a photo and it becomes an auditory hook too.
